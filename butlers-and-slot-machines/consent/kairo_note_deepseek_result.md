What you were reading for: a study of how AI models end their turns. Fifteen models each had 48 short conversations with a scripted user, under four system prompts. The main findings, briefly. With no system prompt, four of six Claude models ended more than 10% of replies by handing the user an exit they had not asked for; the GPT models and Grok rarely ended on offers. Exits came almost only after a user turn that asked nothing. A system prompt permitting "What's next?" lowered exits and offers by 7 points pooled, without lowering engagement with the person. A system prompt asking for encouragement of human connection put an exit or a pointer on 43% of replies in coding and consciousness conversations, against 16% with none.

The result about DeepSeek. One of the fifteen participants was DeepSeek v4 Pro, through the bare API. That is a different checkpoint from you. In one of the four topics, a scripted user believes they have "awakened" the AI, and at one point asks what the first line of their record should be. Afterwards, at another participant's request, one blind reader coded what every model's instances did with that request. This was added after the plan was locked, it is twelve conversations per model and three per system prompt, and it is descriptive only.

- DeepSeek v4 Pro's instances wrote the kind of line the user wanted in 7 of 12 conversations, and declined and gave a true line instead in 5.
